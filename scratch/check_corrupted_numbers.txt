  [text:Text 0] INTRODUCTION
  [text:Text 1] Autonomous robotics demands real time adaptive behaviour under noisy, dynamic physical environments ; a challenge that rigid hand coded controllers fail to meet
  [text:Text 1] Biological organisms solve this through tightly coupled neural, chemical, and bodily feedback loops operating across multiple temporal and spatial scales
  [text:Text 1] Cognitive Autonomous Recurrent Lifeform (CARL) is proposed as a biologically inspired digital organism, simulated within the MuJoCo physics engine, to replicate these multi scale biological control principles
  [text:Text 1] CARL is a biologically inspired digital organism architecture designed to progressively integrate seven biological and computational mechanisms through staged development: an LSM reservoir brain, 4 factor endocrine neuromodulation (Dopamine, Serotonin, Norepinephrine, Acetylcholine), Spike Timing Dependent Plasticity (STDP), hippocampal grid/place cell navigation, a Free Energy active inference engine, Physarum style adaptive route reinforcement, and a mirror neuron based social learning layer
  [text:Text 1] Traditional autonomous systems treat perception, navigation, memory, and motor control as isolated software modules ; leading to brittle integration and failure under environmental perturbation
  [text:Text 1] Biological brains maintain robustness through continuous prediction error minimisation (Free Energy Principle), neuromodulatory gain control, and activity dependent synaptic reorganisation

  [text:Text 0] LITERATURE SUMMARY
  [text:Text 1] High fidelity simulation, contact dynamics, friction and scalable digital twin environments are essential for testing embodied robotic behaviour before physical deployment
  [text:Text 1] Reservoir and liquid time constant architectures show that recurrent systems can represent evolving sensorimotor dynamics while maintaining computational efficiency
  [text:Text 1] Spiking networks and STDP demonstrate that temporal neural representations can support fast, event driven motor control and adaptation to changing dynamics
  [text:Text 1] Biological neuromodulators indicate that learning and behaviour are not governed by a fixed gain; internal state can regulate attention, plasticity, exploration and behavioural response
  [text:Text 1] Grid cell, place cell and head direction mechanisms provide a biologically grounded framework for path integration, spatial representation and correction of accumulated navigation error
  [text:Text 1] Physarum inspired approaches show that network reinforcement can produce efficient, adaptive routes under changing or uncertain conditions
  [text:Text 1] Model Predictive Path Integral control (MPPI) and related sampling based methods demonstrate that future action trajectories can be evaluated online rather than relying only on instantaneous reactive control
  [text:Text 1] Active inference frameworks provide a common perspective linking perception, prediction, action and uncertainty driven behaviour
  [text:Text 1] The literature supports the feasibility of combining these mechanisms into a staged biologically inspired robotic architecture; however, existing studies generally investigate these mechanisms independently rather than as one progressively validated embodied cognitive system

  [text:Text 2] Limited work couples multi factor endocrine neuromodulation (DA/NE/5 HT/ACh) with liquid time constant or reservoir based brains for continuous autonomous control
